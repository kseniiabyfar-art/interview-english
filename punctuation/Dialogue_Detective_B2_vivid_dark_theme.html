05

Quotation-style switch: American vs British 8 min

Practise the two conventions side by side. These are common patterns, not an absolute rule for every publisher: British publications can use double quotation marks or American-style punctuation too.

American English · common style

“Double first”

Direct speech: “I can’t come,” Mia said.

Quoted word: She called the ending “unexpected.”

Nested quote: “She said, ‘Don’t wait.’”

Commas and periods generally go inside the closing quotation marks.

British English · common logical style

‘Single first’

Direct speech: ‘I can’t come,’ Mia said.

Quoted word: She called the ending ‘unexpected’.

Nested quote: ‘She said, “Don’t wait.”’

Punctuation not belonging to the quoted words often goes outside the closing marks.

Important distinction: in direct dialogue before a speech tag, the comma normally stays inside the quotation marks in both styles: “I can’t come,” Mia said. / ‘I can’t come,’ Mia said. The most visible difference is often punctuation after a quoted fragment inside a larger sentence.

Try the style switch yourself

Rewrite the sentence once in American style and once in British logical punctuation. Do not capitalise the quoted phrase because it is part of the surrounding sentence.

Base sentence: the reviewer called the ending surprisingly clever and i agreed

Sample answers

American: The reviewer called the ending “surprisingly clever,” and I agreed.

British logical punctuation: The reviewer called the ending ‘surprisingly clever’, and I agreed.

The comma joins two independent clauses and belongs to the surrounding sentence. In standard American style it goes inside the closing quote; in logical punctuation it goes outside because it is not part of the quoted words. British publishers vary, so follow the style requested by the publication or teacher.

06

Repair the dialogue, then convert the style 10 min

Add quotation marks, commas, apostrophes and capital letters. Make the speech tags lowercase where they continue the same sentence. Then rewrite it with the other quotation-mark convention.

Original message

mia texted i cant come to the screening tonight
why asked alex
my brothers laptop is broken and he wont let me use his headphones however ill try to come next week mia replied
okay ill save you a seat but dont be late alex said

Checklist before you start

Look for contractions (can't, won't, I'll, don't), possession (brother's laptop), a comma before a speech tag, a question mark inside the quotation, the comma after however, and an em dash for emphasis if it fits naturally.

Try both versions before opening the model.

One possible answer

American English

Mia texted, “I can’t come to the screening tonight.”

“Why?” Alex asked.

“My brother’s laptop is broken, and he won’t let me use his headphones. However, I’ll try to come next week,” Mia replied.

“Okay, I’ll save you a seat—but don’t be late,” Alex said.

British English

Mia texted, ‘I can’t come to the screening tonight.’

‘Why?’ Alex asked.

‘My brother’s laptop is broken, and he won’t let me use his headphones. However, I’ll try to come next week,’ Mia replied.

‘Okay, I’ll save you a seat—but don’t be late,’ Alex said.

  • Brother’s laptop uses a possessive apostrophe; can’t, won’t, I’ll and don’t are contractions.
  • Why? has a question mark inside the quotation marks; Alex asked starts with lowercase because it is a speech tag following a question.
  • However, takes a comma after it. This sentence begins a new spoken sentence, so However is capitalised.
  • The comma before Mia replied separates the spoken words from the speech tag.
  • The em dash in seat—but creates a small emphatic turn. A hyphen would not serve the same function.
  • In this direct-dialogue example, the comma before a speech tag stays inside the quotation marks in both conventions; the main visible difference is the quotation-mark style.

1. Proofread the chat

Correct the message. Keep the meaning, but repair quotation marks, commas, apostrophes and capital letters.

hey sam i cant come to your house today my cousins visiting however ill call you later said mia no problem replied sam ill tell my brother that youre not coming

One possible answer · American English

Mia said, “Hey, Sam, I can’t come to your house today. My cousin’s visiting; however, I’ll call you later.”

“No problem,” replied Sam. “I’ll tell my brother that you’re not coming.”

In this version, Sam is direct address, cousin’s indicates possession or a contraction of cousin is depending on the intended meaning (here, a contraction), however follows a semicolon and takes a comma, and the speech tags use lowercase when they continue the same sentence.

2. Style switch

Rewrite this sentence in American English and British logical punctuation: the critic called the ending surprisingly moving and i agreed

Sample answers

American: The critic called the ending “surprisingly moving,” and I agreed.

British logical punctuation: The critic called the ending ‘surprisingly moving’, and I agreed.

The quote is a fragment inside the writer’s sentence, so it starts lowercase. The clause comma is inside in the common American style and outside in the logical-punctuation version.
